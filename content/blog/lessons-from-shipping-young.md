People often ask how I balance school and building products.

The honest answer: I don't balance them perfectly. I prioritize ruthlessly, protect deep work time, and accept that some weeks one side wins.

## Lesson 1: Momentum beats motivation

Waiting to feel ready is a trap.

The days I make progress are usually the days I start before I feel inspired. Open the laptop. Fix one thing. Ship one improvement. Motivation follows action more often than the reverse.

## Lesson 2: Taste compounds

Every product I've shipped made the next one better.

Not because I suddenly became smarter overnight — because I trained my eye. I notice broken flows faster. I cut features earlier. I care more about how something feels when someone uses it for the first time.

## Lesson 3: Audience is a responsibility

Having people watch your journey is powerful, but it can also pull you toward performance instead of progress.

I try to keep one rule: share after I've done the work, not instead of doing the work.

## Lesson 4: Small systems win

I don't rely on perfect days. I rely on systems:

- a clear weekly shipping goal
- a short list of priorities
- a habit of writing things down
- regular reflection on what actually moved the product forward

## Keep going

You don't need to be older, richer, or more connected to start.

You need a problem worth solving, the courage to ship imperfect work, and the patience to improve in public.

That's the game I'm playing.
