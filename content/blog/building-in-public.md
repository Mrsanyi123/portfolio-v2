I used to think I needed a finished product before I could talk about it.

That mindset slowed me down. Now I share while I'm still figuring things out — half-built features, failed experiments, late-night debugging sessions, and the small wins that don't look impressive until you stack them up.

## The real unlock

Building in public isn't about posting every day for engagement. It's about creating a feedback loop.

When people see what you're working on:
- you get early signal before you waste months
- you stay accountable to yourself
- opportunities find you instead of the other way around

I started with simple updates: what I shipped, what broke, and what I learned. No perfect threads. No polished launch videos. Just honest progress.

## What actually works

Here's the approach that stuck for me:

1. **Ship something small every week.** A changelog, a UI polish, a bug fix — progress compounds.
2. **Explain the why.** People connect with decisions, not just screenshots.
3. **Document the messy parts.** The failed API call or redesign is often more useful than the highlight reel.
4. **Stay consistent, not perfect.** Missing a day is fine. Quitting the habit isn't.

## If you're scared to start

Start smaller than you think. Write one short note about something you built this week. Share one lesson from a bug that wasted your afternoon. Post one screenshot of a feature that finally works.

You don't need a huge audience. You need a trail of work that proves you're someone who ships.

I'm still early. Still learning. Still building. And somehow, that honesty has become the most valuable part of the journey.
