I grew up watching people build on the internet from San Francisco, London, Lagos, Bangalore. For a long time it felt like a place I could watch, not a place I could stand in.

Then I started posting the work. Not the highlight reel ΓÇö the actual building. That changed more than any tutorial.

## Context is a feature

"16, Ethiopia, building products" is not a disclaimer. It is information.

People do not follow me because I pretend to be a generic Silicon Valley founder. They follow because the path is specific: school in Addis, products like Shiplog and YScroll, a community that is still being built here.

If you flatten your story to sound global, you become easier to ignore.

## Post the work, not the performance

Building in public fails when it becomes a second product. You start optimizing for screenshots of metrics instead of the thing you were supposed to ship.

My rule is simple. If I cannot point to a commit, a user conversation, or a decision, I do not post yet.

The audience is a side effect. The work is the feed.

## The map is smaller than it looks

A lot of the people I learn from have never been to Ethiopia. A lot of the people I want to help have never been to a tech conference. Both can be true on the same internet.

I want this blog, the videos, and the products to work for both rooms: the person shipping from a well-funded office, and the person shipping after school on bad wifi.

If you are building from a place the timeline forgets, start anyway. The internet is crowded. It is not full.
