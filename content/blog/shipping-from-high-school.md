People love the story. "He is still in high school and he ships products." It sounds like a superpower. It is mostly calendars and saying no.

The constraint is not talent. It is time that does not belong to you. Classes, homework, family, sleep. The work has to fit in the leftover hours, or it does not happen.

## Treat the leftover hours like a job

I used to wait until I "felt like coding." That version of me shipped almost nothing.

The version that works looks boring:

- Pick one product. Not five.
- Give it a daily slot, even if the slot is ninety minutes.
- End the day with a next action written down, so tomorrow does not start with a blank page.

Inspiration is a bonus. The schedule is the product.

## School is not the enemy

I wasted a year treating school like it was in the way. It is not. It is a constraint, and constraints make better taste.

You cannot rebuild the whole app on a Tuesday night. So you learn to cut scope. You ship the smallest thing that is still real. That skill is worth more than another framework.

## Take yourself seriously, not your ego

Taking yourself seriously means you answer emails, you write the changelog, you talk to users, you finish the unsexy 20%.

It does not mean you pretend you have a thirty-person company. I am a teenager in Addis Ababa with a laptop. That is enough, as long as the work is honest.

If you are in school and building, you do not need a bigger origin story. You need a calendar, a product, and a bias toward shipping the next real version.
